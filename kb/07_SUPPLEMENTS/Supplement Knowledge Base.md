## Why Personalized Supplements

### Why standard supplements fail

Standard supplements give the same dosage to a 50 kg woman and a 250 kg man — a clearly inadequate approach. Of 20 standard micronutrients in an off-the-shelf supplement, statistically one is likely ineffective for any given individual, one may actually be harmful, and the rest are probably the wrong dose. Personalization solves all three problems by adjusting which nutrients are included, which form is used, and the exact dose — based on the individual's genetics, blood values, and lifestyle.

### How genes affect nutrient needs

Genes influence micronutrient needs in three concrete ways. Conversion blockers: certain gene variants prevent nutrients from being converted into their active forms (e.g. MTHFR can block conversion of folic acid; some CYP variants block conversion of CoQ10). Healthy-to-harmful switches: genes can make beneficial substances problematic for specific individuals (e.g. iron in certain genotypes; Omega-3 in APOA1 variants). Higher dose requirements: some gene variants mean a person needs significantly higher doses for the same effect (e.g. selenium, Vitamin D3).

### The role of {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}}

{{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} is the solution to one-size-fits-all supplementation. It is formulated individually for each customer based on their genetic results, blood-test results, and lifestyle questionnaire. The customer receives exactly what their body needs, in the right form and the right dose — and excludes nutrients that would be ineffective or harmful for their specific profile.

## Manufacturing Process

### Step 1 — Source and quality-test raw materials

Raw materials are sourced from selected suppliers worldwide. Every incoming batch undergoes a full quality test panel before use: microbiological testing, nutrient content analysis, doping-substance screening, and IR identity verification. Retention samples of every batch are kept for further laboratory analysis if needed.

### Step 2 — Microencapsulate active ingredients

Approved raw materials are mixed with natural fillers (microcrystalline cellulose and corn starch) to form a dough using proprietary in-house technology. The dough is forced under approximately 9 tons of pressure through a fine plate to produce continuous "spaghetti" strands, which are then shaped in a vortex into 1.6–2 mm spherical beads — the {{PELLET_NAME}}. The beads are vacuum-dried and coated with shellac (for stomach-acid resistance) and beeswax (for surface and taste). Individual pellet types are configured for either fast release or slow release depending on the nutrient's requirements.

### Step 3 — Determine nutrient needs

The formulation algorithm processes all available analysis results and questionnaire data for the customer and determines the optimal dose for each of the 20+ nutrients. This includes choosing the appropriate chemical form of each nutrient where multiple options exist (for example, standard folic acid vs. methylfolate based on MTHFR status).

### Step 4 — Calculate the individual recipe

The algorithm converts the nutritional needs profile into a precise recipe specifying which pellet types and how much of each are needed for this customer's 3-month supply. The recipe accounts for fast-release vs. slow-release pellets and for which nutrients should be co-released or kept apart.

### Step 5 — Create the personalized mix

Individual pellet types are weighed and combined in exact proportions for each customer. Dosing precision is at the milligram level, which would be impossible to achieve reliably with conventional capsules or tablets.

### Step 6 — Fill, label, and pack

The personalized mixture is filled into daily {{SACHET_NAME}}s, labelled with the customer's name and recipe details, and packed into a 3-month dispenser. Every sachet is uniquely produced for the customer it is shipped to.

### Step 7 — Ship to customer

The completed 3-month supply is shipped directly to the customer or to the reseller for pickup, depending on the reseller's delivery configuration. Shipments are typically dispatched within days of order completion.

### Where production takes place

All {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} production takes place at Novogenia's own facility in Eugendorf bei Salzburg, Austria. Nothing is outsourced — sourcing, microencapsulation, mixing, filling, labelling, and dispatch are all performed in-house under ISO 22000 food-safety quality management.

## The {{PELLET_NAME}} Technology

### What are {{PELLET_NAME}}?

{{PELLET_NAME}} (also called micro-transporter pellets or microbeads) are 1.6–2 mm spherical beads made from natural plant fibres (microcrystalline cellulose) and corn starch. They are not digested — the beads themselves pass through and are excreted, acting like dietary fibre. The nutrients are carried inside and released as the beads travel through the gastrointestinal tract. Seeing the pellets in stool is normal and expected.

### Fast-release pellets

Fast-release {{PELLET_NAME}} release approximately 70% of their nutrient payload within the first 10 minutes in intestinal conditions, reaching near-complete release within 60 minutes. After ingestion, solutes from fast-release pellets reach the small intestine within roughly 1.5 hours and are absorbed quickly.

### Slow-release pellets

Slow-release {{PELLET_NAME}} release their payload gradually, creating an extended intestinal delivery window of up to 12 hours after ingestion. This is essential for nutrients the body cannot store (such as Vitamin C), and for nutrients that benefit from steady availability rather than a single peak.

### Mixed formulations

A combined mix of fast-release and slow-release {{PELLET_NAME}} produces continuous, steady nutrient delivery throughout the full intestinal delivery window of up to 12 hours. This avoids both the abrupt peaks of conventional capsules and the lag of pure slow-release formats.

### Vitamin C application

Vitamin C is delivered exclusively in slow-release {{PELLET_NAME}}. The body cannot store Vitamin C, so the goal is sustained availability across the day rather than a single peak. Continuous release matches physiological need much more closely than a once-daily dose would.

### Zinc and calcium separation

Zinc and calcium compete for the same absorption pathway in the intestine. By placing them in separate pellets that release in different zones of the gut, {{PELLET_NAME}} technology avoids absorption interference — so both minerals reach the bloodstream rather than one blocking the other.

### Calcium and Vitamin D3 synergy

Calcium and Vitamin D3 are placed in the same pellet so they are released simultaneously. Vitamin D3 is required for the body to absorb calcium effectively. Releasing them together captures this synergistic effect — something a stack of separate capsules cannot reliably achieve.

### Fat-soluble vitamins

Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) need dietary fat to be absorbed. Their {{PELLET_NAME}} are timed to release in contact with the dietary fats from food eaten with the {{SACHET_NAME}}. Taking the sachet with a meal that contains some fat is therefore important for these specific nutrients.

### Physiological basis

The stomach regulates what passes into the duodenum based on physical form. Solids under 3 mm can pass post-prandially alongside food, whereas larger particles are retained until interdigestive contractions. At 1.6–2 mm, {{PELLET_NAME}} behave as small solids — slow-release beads pass with the meal and deliver nutrients across the full intestinal transit, while fast-release beads release quickly upon entering the small intestine.

### What {{PELLET_NAME}} are not

{{PELLET_NAME}} are not tablets or capsules. Only the nutrients are absorbed — the bead itself is excreted intact and acts as dietary fibre. The technology is not patented; it is a proprietary in-house Novogenia development. One daily {{SACHET_NAME}} of {{PELLET_NAME}} is equivalent to approximately 10–15 conventional capsules in nutrient content.

### Pellet colours and taste

The varying colours of the {{PELLET_NAME}} come entirely from the natural pigmentation of the raw materials inside them. No artificial colorants are added. The beeswax coating neutralizes the bitter or metallic tastes of some raw materials, so the pellets are taste-neutral when swallowed.

## Raw Material Philosophy

### Biological versus pharmaceutical sources

Approximately 80% of the raw materials used in {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} come from biological sources (plants, algae, fermentation); approximately 20% come from pharmaceutical synthesis. The split is optimized per-nutrient — not a blanket policy. The goal is the best outcome for the customer rather than adherence to a single sourcing principle.

### When biological sources are preferred

Biological sources are used whenever they can provide adequate concentration, purity, and stability for the nutrient in question. They generally offer better tolerance and higher bioavailability, and align with the philosophy of using forms closer to those found in healthy food.

### When pharmaceutical sources are required

Pharmaceutical synthesis is used where biological sources cannot deliver the required dose, purity, or consistency. Pharmaceutical sources typically offer higher concentration and greater stability, which is essential for nutrients that need precise dosing or that are difficult to extract reliably from natural sources.

### Quality standards for all raw materials

All raw materials in {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}}, regardless of source, are halal certified, kosher certified, hypoallergenic, GMO-free, gluten-free, lactose-free, free of preservatives and artificial colorants, and tested per batch for doping substances. These standards apply uniformly across biological and pharmaceutical inputs.

### Per-batch testing protocol

Every incoming batch of every raw material undergoes microbiological testing, nutrient content analysis, doping-substance screening, and IR identity verification before being approved for use. Retention samples are stored so that any concern arising later can be traced back to the specific batch. Production runs under an ISO 22000 certified quality-management system.

## Personalization Logic

### Why not one standard dose

A 50 kg woman and a 250 kg man have fundamentally different nutrient requirements just from body size alone. Beyond that, genetics creates further individual variation: gene variants can block nutrient conversion (e.g. MTHFR for folic acid), make nutrients harmful for specific people (e.g. iron-overload risk genes), or require higher doses for equivalent effect (e.g. VDR for Vitamin D3). A single standard recipe cannot meet all these realities at once.

### What is personalized

Personalization in {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} covers four dimensions: which nutrients are included (not all nutrients are relevant for all people), which form of each nutrient is used (for example, folic acid vs. methylfolate based on MTHFR; standard vs. vegan D3 based on customer need), the dose of each nutrient calculated to 1 mg precision, and the release profile (fast vs. slow, and which nutrients are co-released).

### What is not personalized

Nutrients without a scientific basis for genetic personalization are not included in {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}}. This is intentional: if the evidence does not support individual variation, a standardized supplement makes more sense for that nutrient and is left out of the personalized formulation. The philosophy is "personalize where the science supports it, not for its own sake."

### The formulation algorithm

The formulation algorithm comprises over 200,000 lines of code. It integrates all available analysis data and produces a recipe specifying the exact composition of the customer's {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} mix. The recipe is recalculated with each new order, incorporating the customer's current age and any new scientific findings added since the last update. The algorithm is improved approximately once per week on average.

### Combinatorial scale of personalised formulas

Across all input dimensions (genetic markers, blood values, lifestyle questionnaire, EFSA upper limits, dose granularity), the personalisation algorithm spans more than 700 trillion possible nutrient combinations. In practice this means no two customer recipes are alike — every dose is generated fresh per order rather than picked from a finite menu of presets.

### DNA-guided IV therapy (clinic channel)

The same genetic dataset that drives {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} can also inform in-clinic IV nutrient infusions: dosing for B-vitamins, magnesium, glutathione, and similar IV nutrients can be aligned to the customer's metabolism, detoxification, and absorption variants. IV delivery is offered by clinical partners; {{COMPANY_NAME}} itself supplies the underlying genetic recipe rather than the IV service.

## Safety and Long-Term Use

### EFSA upper-limit compliance

{{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} is formulated so that even when combined with a nutrient-rich diet and taken every day, total intake never reaches the upper safe limit for any nutrient as defined by the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA). The personalization algorithm includes EFSA limits as hard constraints — no individual dose can exceed them.

### Drug interactions

{{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} simulates the effect of a healthy, optimized diet. Drug interactions are not known and have not been reported. Customers who wish to share their formulation with their doctor can do so — the report includes the exact ingredients and dosages, making it straightforward for a clinician to assess against any prescribed medication.

### Don't double up on nutrients

Do not combine {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} with other supplements containing the same active ingredients. Doing so would exceed the personalized dosage calculation and could push intake above safe limits for some nutrients. If the customer wants additional supplementation, the right approach is to update their analyses or lifestyle questionnaire so the next reformulation accounts for it.

### Colonoscopy guidance

Discontinue {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} approximately one week before a scheduled colonoscopy. This allows the intestine to clear of the {{PELLET_NAME}} beads, which would otherwise remain visible during the procedure. Resume the supplement normally after the colonoscopy is complete.

### Pregnancy guidance

Switch to a standardized prenatal supplement during pregnancy. The reason is medical priority: embryo health depends on standardized prenatal dosing patterns, and personalization based on the mother's individual genetic profile is not the appropriate framework during this period. Resume {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} after pregnancy if desired.

### Reporting concerns

No cases of vitamins and minerals from {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} causing negative health effects have been reported to date. Any customer who experiences symptoms they suspect may be related to the supplement should contact the reseller's customer support so the formulation can be reviewed and, if necessary, adjusted in the next reorder.
