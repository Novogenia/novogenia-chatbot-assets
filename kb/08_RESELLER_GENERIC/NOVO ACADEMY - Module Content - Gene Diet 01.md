## Headline frame

### The 80%-genetic claim

"Es wird wirklich geschätzt, dass ungefähr 80 % des Übergewichts eine genetische Ursache hat. Das heißt, wir sind nicht ganz alleine schuld durch fehlende Disziplin — unsere Gene reden ein Wörtchen mit und arbeiten zum Teil sehr gegen uns." — direct from Daniel, scientific-basis module.

### The calorie-balance basics

The standard calorie balance — "if 3,000 calories come in but only 1,500 are burned, the surplus drives weight gain" — is what most nutritional advice is based on. It makes sense at the basic level: where else would those calories go? But it does not explain the well-known fact that some people can eat as much as they want and not gain weight.

### Why personal disposition matters

Scientists wanted to understand why a calorie surplus produces obesity in some people but not in others. The answer is genetic regulation of which calories actually get absorbed and stored.

## The Volk study — fat calories and weight gain

### Study setup

Volk recruited 720 participants and instructed them to continue eating normally but to add extra fat calories on top. Unsurprisingly, most participants gained weight. The surprising part: some did not.

### What explained the difference

When the body processes a fat-rich meal, it initially welcomes the energy and starts absorbing the fat. At a certain point, **fat-absorption regulator genes** activate and signal "enough" — further fat absorption is blocked and the surplus stays in the gut and is excreted. The key gene is **PPAR-Gamma**.

### Genetic defects in the regulator

In some people, scientists identified inherited defects in these regulator genes — a "spelling error" passed down from parents — that prevents the signal from working. Fat is fully absorbed and stored for later. These individuals are "fat-sensitive". The spectrum runs from fully fat-resistant (cannot get fat from dietary fat) to highly fat-sensitive (every gram of dietary fat counts).

### How many genes are involved

Science has identified **five relevant fat-sensitivity genes** so far, each with a fat-resistant or fat-sensitive variant.

## The three-studies minimum standard

### Why this matters for the conversation

In medical genetics, an effect is considered scientifically established only when at least **three different studies, by three different research groups, on three different populations** have independently confirmed it. {{COMPANY_NAME}} applies this minimum standard rigorously.

### Why some competitors test "more" genes

Some companies in the field advertise "more genes" — but this is only achievable if one accepts single-study or weak evidence. {{COMPANY_NAME}} explicitly does not, which is why the catalog is narrower but more defensible.

## Carbohydrates and protein

### Carbohydrate genetics

Carbohydrate sensitivity is also under genetic control. Some people gain weight from carbohydrates, others do not. Daniel's own example: he is carbohydrate-resistant but strongly fat-sensitive.

### Protein genetics — currently no identified differences

For protein, no genetic differences in handling have been identified to date — at least not at the three-studies-minimum standard.

### Translating to a personal strategy

The ratios of fat- vs carb-sensitivity translate directly into the personal eating strategy: "For me — more spaghetti, more carbs, less steak. Reduce fat, raise carbs. For my wife it is exactly the opposite."

## Exercise — responder vs non-responder

### What 25 studies show

A consolidated review of 25 studies on identical exercise programmes shows the group splits into two categories: **responders** (excellent weight-loss outcomes) and **non-responders** (2× to 8× lower outcomes for the same calories burned). The difference is genetic, not effort.

### Genes involved

ADRB3, FTO, PPAR-Gamma and others.

### The same effect for calorie reduction

For calorie restriction the same pattern applies: some people get up to **3× less weight-loss benefit** from the same calorie deficit. Also genetic.

## Personal strategy options

### Sport-responder + hunger-non-responder (Daniel's profile)

Intensive exercise programme is the lever; calorie reduction (hungering) brings little. Focus on what works.

### Sport-non-responder + hunger-responder (Daniel's wife)

Calorie reduction is the lever; exercise alone won't move the scale enough.

### Mixed profiles

Any blend in between — the recipe is to **focus on the personal strengths and avoid the weaknesses**, rather than apply the same strategy to everyone.

## Validation study — the 2.4× claim

### Study design

300 participants. Phase 1 (3 weeks): standard diet from the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Ernährung — no genetics. Phase 2 (2 weeks pause). Phase 3 (3 weeks): personalised genetic programme.

### Results

| Phase | Programme | Mean weight loss |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (3 weeks) | Standard DGE diet | **1.4 kg** |
| 3 (3 weeks) | Gene-personalised programme | **3.5 kg** |

## The teaching frame for resellers

### Core philosophy

"Wir konzentrieren uns auf die Stärken und meiden die Schwächen." — Use your customer's genetic strengths; work around their weaknesses. The personalised programme is not about restriction — it is about precision.

### Why personalisation drives compliance

The 11% adherence advantage on the personalised programme is itself a major reason it works. People follow plans that feel made for them; they fall off plans that feel generic. This is part of the personalisation effect, not separate from it.

### What this is NOT

It is not a weight-loss promise. The 2.4–2.5× figure is study-derived for the 3-week window. Long-term success still requires the customer doing the work — the programme just makes the work more effective by removing the wasted-effort component.

## Concrete report-reading examples (from module 02 — Beratungsschulung)

### How to read the gene table

The gene table on each customer report lists the analysed genes with their rs-numbers (variant IDs), the customer's genotype (actual lab result), and a function indicator: green = functional, red = impaired (gene defect or unfavourable variant). The rs-number is NOT a score — it is the database ID for the variant.

### How to read fat-sensitivity values

A "moderate fat-sensitivity" finding does not mean "avoid fat completely". The final macronutrient recommendation depends on BOTH fat-sensitivity AND carb-sensitivity. The two readings are interpreted together.

### How to translate "low carb-sensitivity + high fat-sensitivity"

"Spaghetti, rice and bread are not your main problem. Reduce fat instead — and when you eat fat, prefer healthy fat sources." Concrete, not punitive.

### How to handle the "I can eat 47% carbs?" question

That is the recommended ideal calorie distribution, not a list of permitted foods. A balanced mix of carbohydrate sources (vegetables, wholegrain, legumes) remains important.

### How to explain the partner comparison

If two people get different recommendations from "the same report", explain that the reports are not the same — the underlying genetic profiles are different, so the optimal macronutrient distribution is different for each.

### How to handle weak satiety

For a customer with weak satiety signal: recommend eating more slowly — this gives the satiety mechanism time to build before too much has been eaten. Do NOT recommend ignoring hunger or doubling portion sizes.
