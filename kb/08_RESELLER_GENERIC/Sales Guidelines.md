## General Selling Approach

### Position genetics as personalised, not generic

The fundamental sales argument: standard health advice is generic, but each customer's body is genetically unique. A 50 kg woman and a 250 kg man are given the same supplement dose — that cannot be optimal. Lead with this universal insight, then introduce the specific analysis that addresses the customer's primary concern.

### Use the "logical conclusion" framing

Confirmed genetic traits + logical actions derived from them. Do not over-promise: the analysis itself is scientifically confirmed (≥3 independent studies per gene). The actions derived are logical conclusions in an experimental stage. This framing is both legally defensible and credible.

### Lead with the customer's strongest pain point

Identify the customer's most urgent concern (weight, energy, ageing, performance, skin) and lead with the product that addresses that — usually a {{PACKAGE_LIFESTYLE_PLUS}} subscription which covers the broadest set of needs.

## {{NUTRITION_SENSOR}} — Sales Framework

### Opening questions

"The food pyramid gives the same advice to everyone — yet some people are lactose intolerant, others gluten-sensitive. These are genetic differences. Did you know there are many more such individual differences most people don't know about?" Or: "Have you noticed some supplements seem to work for some people but not others? There's a genetic reason for that."

### Ideal customers

People interested in healthy eating; people with digestive issues; people already taking supplements (often mis-dosed); people with poor cholesterol or homocysteine values; people interested in anti-ageing.

### Gene examples for sales conversations

HFE (iron): some people absorb too much iron — iron supplements harm them. APOA1 (Omega-3): in some variants, Omega-3 worsens cholesterol. MTHFR (folic acid): ~8% cannot activate folic acid without methylfolate. Free-radical protection genes: defective protectors need high-dose antioxidants to compensate.

## {{TOXO_SENSOR}} — Sales Framework

### Opening question

"Some people smoke for decades without problems; others develop lung cancer. The difference isn't in how much they smoke — it's in their detoxification genes."

### Ideal customers

Smokers; frequent grillers; people exposed to chemicals professionally; fish and shellfish lovers; people with amalgam fillings; coffee or alcohol drinkers; anti-ageing focused customers.

### Objection: "I don't need a test to protect myself from toxins"

"Knowing where your weaknesses lie lets you take targeted action. For some, organic food matters enormously; for others not. For some, fish is healthy; for others, it accumulates heavy metals. These are important individual differences."

## Objection Handling — Universal Set

### "Too expensive"

For DNA-based products: genes don't change — €1/month spread across a lifetime is the right framing. For supplements: €3.30–€3.80/day, less than half a coffee. Personalisation prevents future health problems from deficiencies.

### "Data security concerns"

Austrian Federal Ministry of Health authorization for medical genetics; multiple ISO certifications; American CLIA. Strict medical data protection. DNA destroyed after analysis. Data deletion available on request.

### "Is this a reputable company?"

Austria's largest COVID lab at peak (275,000 samples/day); 35+ million PCR tests since 2009; European market leader in nutrigenetics; profitable since 2013; parent company Darwin AG listed on Munich Stock Exchange.

### "Standard supplements are cheaper"

When buying separate products to cover individual needs, total cost quickly exceeds the personalised supplement — without the guarantee of matching the customer's actual needs.

### "I don't need a test to eat healthily"

Lactose intolerance is the obvious example: well-meaning advice (drink milk for calcium) doesn't fit everyone. Everyone has individual genetic rules.

### "Is it scientifically confirmed?"

Every gene confirmed by at least 3 independent studies from 3 different research groups. PubMed sources are cited in the back of every report.

### "I don't need a test to lose weight"

Internal study with 300 people showed 2.5× more weight loss with the genetic program vs. standard recommendations — same effort.

## What NOT to do

### Do not promise specific medical outcomes

Avoid statements like "this will cure" or "this will prevent". Genetic adaptations of dietary and supplement recommendations are logical conclusions, not randomised-trial-proven outcomes for every effect.

### Do not name competitors negatively in writing

Verbal sales conversations have more latitude. Written customer-facing materials should not name competitors negatively — that opens legal risk.

### Do not over-promise on Performance+ for non-athletes

{{PERFORMANCE_SENSOR}} is for intense athletes. Selling it to general customers is wasteful — {{NUTRITION_SENSOR}} or the full {{PACKAGE_LIFESTYLE_PLUS}} is the right fit.

### Do not sell {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} during pregnancy

Pregnancy requires standardised prenatal supplements optimised for embryo development, not personalisation for the mother. Direct pregnant customers to their doctor for a suitable prenatal product.
