## Overview

### What the customer receives

The {{BURNOUT_SENSOR}} report is a roughly 40-page personalised document covering the genetic side of stress vulnerability, recovery, and burnout risk — focused on neurotransmitter breakdown, cortisol response, and sleep biology.

### Tone and framing

Recommendations are written in the second person and explicitly non-medical: the report focuses on stress-management lifestyle strategies, never diagnoses depression or anxiety. Findings are framed as predispositions modifiable by behaviour, sleep, nutrition, and supplementation.

## Report sections

### Front matter

Foreword, "What are genes", brief intro to stress biology, and the standard disclaimer (the report supports stress-management planning; it is not a mental-health diagnosis).

### Results overview

A "Your results at a glance" summary.

### Topic chapters

Catecholamine breakdown (COMT Val158Met), serotonin transport (5-HTT), HPA-axis sensitivity (FKBP5, NR3C1), sleep regulation (CLOCK, PER variants), magnesium and B-vitamin requirements relevant to stress, and a personalised "stress recovery" action panel.

### Closing pages

About-the-laboratory + technical details.

## Chapter structure

### How every chapter is built

Scientific intro, "most important details at a glance" box, gene-function explanation, customer's genotype + verdict, recommendation paragraph, PubMed citations.

### Verdict categories

Three frames: "favourable / typical" (no special strategy), "moderate variant" (mild adjustment recommended), "high-impact variant" (clear lifestyle intervention indicated).

## Example findings

### Example 1 — favourable

Topic: Catecholamine breakdown. Gene: COMT (Val/Val). Verdict: "Fast COMT — efficient catecholamine clearance." Narrative: the customer clears stress hormones (adrenaline, dopamine) quickly under load; bounces back from acute stress relatively easily. Tendency to perform well in chaotic environments but may seek stimulation; risk of overwork.

### Example 2 — moderate variant

Topic: Serotonin transport. Gene: 5-HTTLPR. Verdict: "Short allele present." Narrative: associated with stronger emotional reactivity to negative environments but also stronger response to positive interventions (therapy, supportive environments). Recommendation: prioritise sleep, social connection, and structured stress-recovery practices.
