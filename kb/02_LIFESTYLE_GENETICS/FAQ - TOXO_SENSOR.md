### What does the {{TOXO_SENSOR}} analyze?

The {{TOXO_SENSOR}} examines genes involved in Phase II detoxification, particularly the glutathione S-transferase system (GSTM1, GSTP1, GSTT1). These enzymes neutralize foreign substances and free radicals. Phase II enzymes are also partially analyzed in the {{PHARMACO_SENSOR}} (TPMT, NAT2, UGT1A1).

### How do Phase I and Phase II detoxification relate to each other?

Detoxification runs in three phases. Phase I: Cytochrome P450 enzymes chemically modify foreign substances — medications, plasticizers, solvents, pesticides — through oxidation, reduction, or hydrolysis. This makes them more water-soluble but can also generate reactive intermediates that are temporarily more toxic than the original. Phase II: Conjugation enzymes (including the glutathione S-transferase system) attach molecules like glutathione, glucuronic acid, or sulfate to these intermediates, neutralizing their reactivity. Phase III: The conjugated compounds are transported out of cells and excreted via the liver and bile or the kidneys.

### What is the difference between Phase 1 and Phase 2 detoxification processes? Are they sequential, or are they separate mechanisms categorized differently?

Reply: Detoxification is divided into 3 phases, which build on one another. In the first phase, foreign substances such as medications, plasticizers, solvents, and pesticides are broken down by enzymes (cytochromes) and prepared for detoxification phase 2. The resulting metabolic intermediates can be more toxic than the original substance. Therefore, it is especially important that phase 2 follows as quickly as possible to enable the elimination of these substances. Detoxification phase 1 functions relatively well in most people. In the second phase, the substances broken down in phase 1 are rendered harmless by the glutathione system and directed towards excretion. The glutathione system is disrupted in many people, making the elimination of toxins difficult. After the pollutants are metabolized in phase 1 and prepared for elimination in phase 2, the excretion of fat-soluble substances occurs via the liver and bile, while water-soluble substances are excreted via the kidneys (phase 3).

### How significant is the influence of the COMT gene on alcoholism and cannabis consumption?

a. Catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) is an enzyme that deactivates various catecholamines (adrenaline, noradrenaline, and dopamine) and various neuroactive drugs, thus ensuring their breakdown. The polymorphism results in a slowed breakdown. It is important to note: the longer the breakdown takes, the longer the catecholamines act on the organism. b. Using cannabis as an example: THC causes a dopamine imbalance in the brain. It can lead to either a dopamine deficiency (negative symptoms) or a dopamine excess (positive symptoms). If there is an excess, dopamine can only be inactivated very slowly by COMT and acts on the body for too long. The resulting persistent high usually leads to increased consumption, which in turn triggers more problems and dependency. i. A study from 2019 showed that carriers of one or both risk alleles of COMT rs4680 tend to earlier and riskier alcohol and drug use, as this polymorphism, among other things, leads to reduced stress activity, which triggers increased positive feelings. This aligns with the aforementioned findings on COMT and cannabis. ii. Another study from 2020 showed that drug addicts (cannabinoids) carrying the risk allele of COMT rs4680 predominantly tended to stronger psychotic episodes compared to carriers of the normal allele. This is due to the too slow breakdown of, among other things, dopamine by COMT and the resulting feelings.

### My report shows GST as null, but a different genetic test from another company indicates otherwise. Could you clarify whether this is the same result or a different genetic variation?

Null allele is the term for the variant in this case. Otherwise, there is usually always a specific rs-number (global designation of gene variants). In this case, it is called "null allele" because either the entire gene for the resulting enzyme is present (INSert) or not present (DELetion). This means that the enzyme can either be produced or not. At the moment, we can only indicate if it is homozygously present (INS/INS) or not present (DEL/DEL). We are currently working on establishing the test for heterozygosity, i.e., whether one allele with INS and one with DEL (INS/DEL) is present.
