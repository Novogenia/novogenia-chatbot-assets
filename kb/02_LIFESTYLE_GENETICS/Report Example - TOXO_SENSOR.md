## Overview

### What the customer receives

The {{TOXO_SENSOR}} report is a roughly 70-page personalised document covering Phase I and Phase II detoxification, oxidative stress, and substance-handling genes. Foreword + "what genes are" + disclaimer, results overview, dedicated chapter per detox topic, lab + technical closing.

### Tone and framing

Recommendations are written in the second person and tied back to the customer's genotype. The report frames genotype as a risk multiplier on exposure rather than a deterministic verdict.

## Report sections

### Front matter

Foreword, "What are genes", "When genes do not work correctly", and the disclaimer. Covers how Phase I, Phase II, and Phase III detoxification interact, and why genotype only matters when exposure is present.

### Results overview

A "Your results at a glance" summary listing every topic with the headline finding in one line.

### Topic chapters

Detoxification of burned/grilled substances (CYP1A1, CYP1B1), detoxification of industrial chemicals and pesticides (GSTM1, GSTT1, GSTP1), heavy-metal handling, caffeine and alcohol metabolism (CYP1A2, ADH, ALDH2), oxidative-stress defence (SOD2, GPX1, CAT, NQO1), and a personalised "what to avoid" / "what to favour" summary.

### Closing pages

About-the-laboratory page, technical details (methodology, accreditations), and source citations.

## Chapter structure

### How every chapter is built

Each chapter follows the same template: scientific introduction, "most important details at a glance" box, what the genes do when functional vs defective, the customer's genotype with rs-IDs and verdict, recommendation paragraph, and a "what to avoid" / "what to favour" panel. PubMed citations close each chapter.

### Verdict categories

Three frames: "functional" (no special intervention), "impaired" (moderate exposure-reduction recommended), "defective" (clear avoidance strategy + supportive nutrients).

## Example findings

### Example 1 — functional gene

Topic: Caffeine breakdown. Gene: CYP1A2. Verdict: "Fast caffeine metabolism." Narrative: the customer clears caffeine normally; moderate coffee intake is well tolerated.

### Example 2 — impaired Phase I activator

Topic: Detoxification of grilled foods. Gene: CYP1A1 (high-activity variant). Verdict: "PAH activation elevated." Narrative: PAHs from charred/grilled meat are activated more efficiently into DNA-reactive intermediates. Recommendation: limit charred and smoked foods; cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, kale) support balanced Phase I/II metabolism.

### Example 3 — null Phase II conjugator

Topic: Industrial chemicals. Gene: GSTM1-null + GSTT1-null. Verdict: "Reduced glutathione conjugation capacity." Narrative: the customer has limited reserve for clearing pesticides, solvents, and heavy-metal metabolites. Recommendation: thoroughly wash and peel produce (prefer organic where possible), ventilate during solvent use, limit high-mercury fish; sulforaphane (broccoli sprouts) and N-acetylcysteine support glutathione.

### Example 4 — defective antioxidant gene

Topic: Oxidative stress defence. Gene: SOD2 (Val/Val). Verdict: "Mitochondrial superoxide handling impaired." Narrative: reduced mitochondrial antioxidant capacity. Recommendation: dietary antioxidants (berries, dark chocolate, polyphenol-rich oils), manganese as the obligatory cofactor for MnSOD, plus CoQ10 for mitochondrial support.

### Example 5 — at-risk variant, lifestyle

Topic: Alcohol handling. Gene: ALDH2 (East Asian variant). Verdict: "Acetaldehyde clearance impaired." Narrative: acetaldehyde accumulates after alcohol intake, producing flushing and elevated long-term cancer risk. Recommendation: minimise alcohol intake; the report explicitly notes this is a strong-effect variant.
