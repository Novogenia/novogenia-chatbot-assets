## {{NUTRITION_SENSOR}} — Food List & Recommendations

### What do the apple symbols (smileys) in the {{FOOD_TABLE_NAME}} mean?

The apple symbols indicate whether a food is rated predominantly healthy (green apples) or predominantly unhealthy (red apples) based on the genetic analysis. The system evaluates over 40 nutritional components of each food against approximately 20 metabolic risk factors derived from the genes. More green = more beneficial ingredients. Red foods are not forbidden, but should be eaten less frequently.

### What do the weight symbols in the {{FOOD_TABLE_NAME}} mean?

The weight symbols indicate whether the macronutrient distribution (fat, carbohydrates, protein) of a food is favorable (green) or unfavorable (red) for that person's weight loss goals. The calculation also considers the calories per typical serving.

### Why can coffee be rated positively while caffeine is rated negatively?

Coffee contains many antioxidants (health-promoting) AND caffeine (which can negatively affect bone and heart health). Depending on genetic predisposition, one aspect outweighs the other. Decaffeinated coffee is a good alternative for slow caffeine metabolizers.

### Are red-rated foods completely forbidden?

No. Red foods should be the exception rather than the rule, but are not forbidden. Heavily red-rated foods (4–6 symbols) should be eaten less frequently than moderately red-rated ones (1–3 symbols).

### Why are similar foods rated differently?

Even similar foods can have different ingredients or different distributions of nutrients. Preparation method also plays a role (cooking can destroy vitamins). Processed products often have a different composition compared to their raw ingredients.

### Do the quantity specifications refer to raw or cooked weight?

For pasta, rice, bread, cereals, grains, grain products, fruits, potatoes, fish, and meat: raw weight. For canned legumes: drained and rinsed. For "menu components": prepared dishes.

### Can a lactose or gluten intolerance be diagnosed through the genetic analysis?

No. The analysis only identifies an elevated genetic risk of developing an intolerance. Where a risk is present, affected foods are marked with a warning symbol (!). An actual diagnosis requires further clinical testing.

### What is the significance of the glycemic index in the food list evaluation?

The glycemic index (GI) is used to determine the effect of a carbohydrate-rich food on blood sugar levels. The higher the GI value, the more sugar enters the bloodstream after consuming a specific food. Originally, the glycemic index comes from diabetes research. Simply put, you stay full longer if you consume foods with a low GI value. A good example of this would be the difference between whole grain and white bread. When you eat white bread, you're quickly hungry again. This results in you consuming more calories. In contrast, if you eat whole grain bread, your blood sugar level doesn't rise as sharply, and you stay full until your next meal. Diabetics, in particular, should always have a table of the glycemic index of foods on hand. Diabetics are recommended to follow a diet that includes foods with a low glycemic index. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), a diet based on foods with a low glycemic index is also beneficial for the general population. After all, a low-glycemic index diet is said to protect against colon cancer and coronary heart disease. Nowadays, even many endurance athletes swear by a diet with a low glycemic index. It is also interesting to observe that the actual blood sugar response strongly depends on the combination of foods consumed together in a meal. The sum of the GI values of individual foods plays a minor role. Much more important are the ingredients of the foods and their interactions. The glycemic index is not entirely straightforward, as there are significant individual variations. The same food can cause different increases in blood sugar levels in different people. ### What is the difference between the glycemic index and glycemic load?

In contrast to the glycemic index, which determines the effect of a carbohydrate-containing food on blood sugar levels, the glycemic load indicates the carbohydrate density of a food. Thus, two different foods with the same or similar GI value can have differing GL values. Therefore, with the same or similar GI value, the food with a lower GL value causes a smaller increase in blood sugar levels.
