## Headline result

### The 2.5× claim, in one sentence

Across all 303 participants, the genetic-personalised diet produced 2.5× greater body-weight loss in 3 weeks than the same participants achieved on standard German nutritional-society guidelines, with high statistical significance (p<0.001).

### Why a customer should care

The study compared the same person against themselves on two different diets — first the standard advice, then the personalised genetic plan — which removes the usual problem of comparing different people. The 2.5× difference therefore reflects the contribution of personalisation itself, not differences in motivation, body type, or starting weight.

---

## Study design

### Who was tested

303 adults aged 19 to 86 (51 men, 252 women), recruited via internet and magazine advertisements. Mean age 41.5 years (SD 13), mean body weight 84 kg (SD 18), mean BMI 29.4 kg/m² (SD 5.4). Eighteen participants had previously diagnosed hypothyroidism — included rather than excluded to test whether the genetic approach worked in a typical real-world group.

### Sequential intervention design

Each participant served as their own control. Phase 1: participants received the standard nutrition booklet from the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Ernährung (the basis of mainstream German nutritional counselling) and followed it for 3 weeks. Phase 2: a 2-week washout in which they returned to normal eating. Phase 3: participants received their personalised genetic results and recommendations in writing and followed the genetic plan for 3 weeks. Body weight and adherence were recorded weekly throughout.

### What "the genetic diet" meant in this study

The genetic plan was built from seven gene variants known to modify diet response: FABP2 (rs1799883), PPARG (rs1801282), ADRB2 (rs1042714), ADRB3 (rs4994), APOA2 (rs5082), APOA5 (rs662799), and FTO (rs9939609). Each participant's combined genotype determined a "fat score" and a "carb score", which in turn shifted the macronutrient distribution of their personalised plan (carbs typically 45–49%, fat typically 31–35%, protein held at 20%).

## Results

### Whole-group result

Mean weight loss on the standard diet was 1.49 kg in 3 weeks. Mean weight loss on the genetic diet was 3.65 kg in 3 weeks — a 2.5-fold difference (p<0.001).

### Results by entry BMI

Normal-weight participants (BMI 19–24.99) benefited most: 0.87 kg standard vs 2.86 kg genetic = 3.3-fold. Overweight participants (BMI 25–30): 1.49 kg vs 3.45 kg = 2.3-fold. Obese participants (BMI >30): 1.79 kg vs 4.26 kg = 2.4-fold. All three subgroups reached p<0.001.

### Results by sex

Men lost more total weight than women on both diets (mean 4.6 kg on the genetic diet) but showed a smaller relative advantage from personalisation (1.9-fold vs the standard diet). Women showed a larger relative benefit, consistent with men responding well to standard "eat less, move more" advice and women benefiting disproportionately from the personalised approach.

### Result in hypothyroid subgroup

Participants with previously diagnosed hypothyroidism — a group commonly considered "difficult" for weight-loss programmes — also benefited: 1.6 kg standard vs 3.3 kg genetic, a 2.1-fold advantage, p<0.001.

### Compliance difference

Participants adhered 11.8% more strictly to the personalised genetic recommendations than to the standard plan. The authors call this out explicitly in the discussion: compliance is one of the biggest problems in weight-loss programmes, and the personalised plan's motivational advantage is a real (and intended) part of why it works better.

## How to discuss this with a customer

### What the bot can confidently say

"In our internal study with 303 participants, the personalised genetic diet produced about 2.5 times more weight loss than the same participants achieved on standard nutritional advice — over the same 3-week period, in the same people. The benefit was largest for normal-weight participants (3.3×) and was still significant in people with hypothyroidism (2.1×). The study is sequential — every participant was their own control — so the difference reflects the personalisation itself, not group differences."

### Limitations the bot should acknowledge honestly

The study is internal and not yet published in a peer-reviewed journal. Sequential (within-person) design rather than randomised parallel-arm design. Short duration (3 weeks per phase). The 11.8% adherence advantage on the genetic plan means motivation/personalisation are entangled with the genetic recommendations themselves — that is by design (a real-world personalised programme always includes a motivational element), but it is worth naming.

### What this study does NOT claim

It does not claim that {{SUPPLEMENT_DAILY}} or {{SUPPLEMENT_WEIGHT}} themselves drive 2.5× more weight loss — those were not the intervention. It does not claim 2.5× more weight loss over months or years — only the 3-week intervention window. It does not replace lifestyle change with a pill — the genetic plan is still a calorie-controlled nutritional plan, just tuned to the person's genotype.
