## Sample Collection & Tracking

### How do I correctly collect my saliva sample?

{{COMPANY_NAME}} uses a proprietary saliva collection device — not a swab. To collect your sample, swirl a small amount of water around your mouth and spit it into the collection tube provided. Avoid eating for 30 minutes before taking the sample. The device is designed to stabilize the sample across a wide temperature range for extended periods and neutralizes any microorganisms and viruses present, ensuring a clean sample for reliable DNA extraction.

### What happens if my sample quality was insufficient?

One saliva sample is collected; the laboratory can test it up to three times to ensure a valid result. If all three attempts are unsuccessful, a new sample is requested. Common causes of recall or quality issues are: eating shortly before collection, a leaked tube, or an insufficiently filled tube.

### How can I be sure the results actually come from my sample?

Every sample tube already carries a barcode and data matrix label when sent out. On receipt, these are captured by automated systems — eliminating human error in sample tracking. The customer's identity is not visible to laboratory staff during analysis. Only after analysis is complete are the results linked to the customer's data and delivered.

### How are samples and customer data stored and disposed of?

The DNA is destroyed after analysis — no further analyses can be performed on it. Customer data (identity, address, order, consent, lab results) are retained separately in accordance with legal data retention requirements and data protection regulations.

### How are the results delivered?

Delivery options vary by reseller — see the reseller availability matrix for which apply: digital PDF report via download, interactive HTML report (app-like experience), printed physical book, and/or an AI chatbot trained on the customer's individual results. Reports are available in German and English on request.

### What processes ensure the accurate management of customer data for Personalized supplements production, especially concerning changes in customer details like name changes due to marriage?

With each order, it is checked whether this customer already exists under this DC and what they have ordered. All data of this customer is then merged and serves as the basis for the next production. ### How do genetic variations influence micronutrient recommendations based on individual health risks?

Providing nutritional recommendations based on genetic analyses is a complex topic. Most laboratories agree on what certain genes do and which functions in the body need support. Nevertheless, the recommendations differ. This is similar to the advice from two different nutritionists. Both will develop a program that works for you, but the paths to the goal will differ. An example: Vitamin C and Vitamin E are both important antioxidants. If you have a deficiency that requires more antioxidants for compensation, increasing the intake of Vitamin C and/or Vitamin E will have the same effect. Both will support your body when your genes do not, and both methods have a validated impact on your body. Another factor is the scope of the genes tested. When multiple genes that influence a particular function are tested, there are also several ways to influence this function. As far as we know, there are many very popular DNA tests that analyze only 30% of the genes we analyze. So if you have already had an analysis done with another provider who tests fewer genes, the result will be different. Furthermore, there are many providers that always round up your results to the next whole number. So if your analysis with us shows that you need exactly 312mg of folate, another test might already suggest 350mg or 400mg. This often suggests a standardized product where every customer for whom folate is recommended receives a predetermined amount.
