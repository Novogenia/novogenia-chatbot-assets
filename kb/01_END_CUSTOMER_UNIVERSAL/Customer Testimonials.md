## How the bot should use this file

### When a customer asks for social proof

Pick 1–2 quotes that match the customer's specific situation (their goal, their concern, their objection). Lead with the quote in the customer's voice, then explain how it relates to the underlying product. Never paste a wall of testimonials — pick what's relevant.

### Compliance reminders

Individual testimonials describe individual experience. They are not promises of result. The bot should NEVER say "you will lose X kg" or "you will feel Y" based on a testimonial — only "one of our customers reported …". When weight-loss numbers are quoted, the bot should pair them with the 2.5× study result (Internal Weight-Loss Study) which IS the platform-wide evidence base.

### Athlete endorsements

The named athletes below have publicly endorsed the product and can be quoted by name. All other testimonials are first-name + initial only (privacy standard).

## Recurring customer themes

### Theme 1 — Personalisation validates the effort

The most frequent customer reaction across both sources: relief that "finally something is actually made for me" rather than the same one-size-fits-all multivitamin or diet. Customers consistently mention DNA + blood as the differentiator that built their trust.

### Theme 2 — "I can feel the difference when I stop taking it"

Long-term customers (2+ years) frequently mention they pause supplementation occasionally and notice a drop in energy / wellbeing — then resume. This is a powerful retention story for the "+" subscription products.

### Theme 3 — Convenience replaces shelf full of bottles

Customers describe consolidating 10–18 different supplements into 1 daily {{SACHET_NAME}}. One Rootine customer reported $300/month savings and went from "16–18 different vitamins to 1 little packet". Strong against the "isn't it expensive?" objection.

### Theme 4 — Surprise findings change behaviour

Customers report being surprised by specific genetic findings ("I never knew I shouldn't take Omega-3 supplements" / "I always thought carbs were the enemy — turns out they're fine for me"). The bot can use these as illustrations of what {{NUTRITION_SENSOR}} and {{WEIGHT_SENSOR}} actually reveal.

### Theme 5 — Customer service matters

Customers consistently mention support quality. The bot can lean on this when the customer has a process question or hits a friction point (sample mix-up, timing question, etc.).

### Theme 6 — Honest mixed feedback exists

Some customers report difficulty with the time investment, packet-opening, the rigour of the food list, or slow progress in the 3-week window. The bot should be honest about this rather than only quoting glowing reviews — it builds trust.

## How to attribute and qualify

### Quote attribution standards

For DNC weight-loss customers: first name + initial + age + product + result (e.g. "Claudia P. (32), -3,4 kg in 3 Wochen, Weight Sensor"). For Rootine reviewers: first name + initial only (e.g. "Liz M."). For named athletes: full name + professional context (as published).

### Standard disclaimer to pair with any quote

"Individual results vary. {{COMPANY_NAME}}'s genetic traits are scientifically confirmed (≥3 independent studies per gene). The personalised recommendations are logical conclusions drawn from those traits — they are not randomised-placebo-controlled outcomes for every effect. The 2,5-fache higher weight-loss efficiency on the genetic diet is documented in {{COMPANY_NAME}}'s internal study (see Internal Weight-Loss Study)."

### What the bot must NOT do with testimonials

Do not invent quotes. Do not attribute one customer's experience to another customer's situation as if it were guaranteed. Do not promise weight-loss numbers from a specific testimonial — always pair with the study-level "2,5-fache" framing. Do not extend an athlete endorsement to product lines they did not specifically mention.
